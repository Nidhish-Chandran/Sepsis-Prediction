About SepsisLens: Methodology, Architecture & Clinical Scope

Full documentation on the clinical objective, machine learning methodology, and ethical safeguards.

1. The Clinical Problem: Early Sepsis Detection

Sepsis is defined by the international Sepsis-3 consensus as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. In intensive care units worldwide, sepsis remains a primary driver of morbidity, prolonged hospitalization, and mortality.

Clinical studies indicate that each hour of delayed administration of effective antimicrobial therapy during septic shock is associated with an approximate 7.6% increase in mortality. However, early signs of sepsis (subtle tachycardia, respiratory rate fluctuations, lab shifts) mimic general post-operative recovery or other inflammatory conditions, making early detection exceedingly difficult.

The PhysioNet 2019 Sepsis Challenge Definition
The task formulated by the PhysioNet / Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019 is to predict sepsis onset 6 hours before clinical criteria are met (defined by clinical suspicion of infection combined with a ≥2 point increase in SOFA score). This 6-hour forward-shifted window provides clinicians a crucial intervention window.

2. System Architecture & 164-Feature Engineering

Raw ICU records contain 40 raw clinical variables across vitals, labs, and demographics. Due to high sparsity (>90% missingness across key labs like Lactate or Bilirubin), SepsisLens expands the feature representation to 164 dimensions:

Feature Category Count Description & Clinical Rationale
Static & Demographics 6 Age, Gender, ICU unit type (Unit1/Unit2), Hospital Admission Time, and Length of Stay (ICULOS).
Missingness Indicators (_msg) 26 Binary flags recording whether each lab test was absent at that hour, capturing clinician diagnostic intent.
Cumulative Observation Counts (_cnt) 14 Running frequency of tests ordered so far, reflecting patient illness severity.
Forward-Filled Signals 34 Carrying last observed vital sign and lab value forward within each patient's timeline.
6-Hour Rolling Statistics 56 Rolling mean, min, max, and standard deviation over a 6-hour window capturing physiological instability.
Hour-to-Hour Differentials (_d1) 14 First-order discrete derivative reflecting instantaneous physiological velocity.
Baseline Drift (_dbase) 14 Cumulative physiological deviation from the patient's admission baseline state.

Total Engineered Dimensions: 6 + 26 + 14 + 34 + 56 + 14 + 14 = 164 Features.

3. Preprocessing Integrity & Data Leakage Prevention

To guarantee valid generalization, the application enforces the following safeguards:

  • Patient-Level Stratified Splitting: The 5,000-patient cohort was partitioned by patient ID into 70% train (3,500 patients), 15% validation (750 patients), and 15% test (750 patients). No patient's records ever straddle two sets.
  • Strict Training Imputation: The median imputer is fitted strictly on the 133,397 training rows. Validation and test sets are transformed using training medians only.
  • Chronological Forward-Fill: Forward-fills and rolling windows operate strictly forward in time within each patient, preventing temporal leakage.
  • Post-Split Resampling (SMOTE): SMOTE was evaluated strictly after patient splitting on training rows only, exposing the true generalization performance.

4. Clinical Limitations & Ethical Considerations

IMPORTANT REGULATORY NOTICE: SepsisLens is an exploratory academic research software prototype. It has not received FDA clearance, CE mark certification, or prospective clinical trial validation as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD). It must never be used to initiate, alter, or withhold patient treatments.
  • Cross-Hospital Domain Shift: In Cell 33 of the notebook, training on Hospital A and evaluating on unseen Hospital B resulted in a drop in ROC-AUC from 0.8224 to 0.7509, and PR-AUC from 0.1259 to 0.0484. Hospital-specific lab test patterns cause significant domain shift.
  • Alert Fatigue Risk: A single-hour trigger policy produces a 21.5% false alarm rate among non-septic patients. Clinicians should employ a multi-hour confirmation policy (e.g. 2 or 3 consecutive hours) to suppress false positives.
  • Missing Data Sensitivity: When an ICU patient has very few lab tests recorded, the model relies on demographic averages and population medians. Clinicians must inspect the Missing Signal Audit.
  • Uncalibrated Score Representation: The Random Forest output score represents the ensemble vote fraction (the proportion of decision trees voting positive), which is an uncalibrated probability score.
